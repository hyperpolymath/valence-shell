== Purpose

This is a focused re-audit of one thing: *what Valence Shell's prose is allowed
to claim about assurance*, and *what its relationship with JanusKey actually is*.

It is the second audit in the log. It follows
`docs/audits/2026-06-01-deep-audit.adoc`, which set the convention that audits
append rather than rewrite. It changes no proof and adds no theorem. Everything
below is either a measured fact with a command, or a correction to a sentence
that claimed more than the measured facts support.

Five rules were treated as fixed points, and prose was brought back to them:

. Proof, warrant and receipt are different things, and a claim must say which
  one it is making.
. A cross-repo orientation document is *not* a mechanised dependency.
. Reversibility here is *algorithmic*, not thermodynamic.
. The Lean/Coq/etc. model → Rust/POSIX extraction gap is *the* trust boundary.
  It is open.
. RMO is best-effort local overwrite/unlink plus an audit residue. It is not
  physical erasure and not GDPR compliance.

== Verdict in one paragraph

The repository's *headline* claims were already honest: `README.adoc`,
`FAQ.adoc`, `docs/wiki/Verification-Status.md`, `docs/RMO-DURABILITY-BOUNDARY.adoc`
and `docs/PROOF-OPEN-FRONTIER.adoc` all state the extraction gap, decline the
GDPR claim, and separate model proof from implementation. The drift was in the
*secondary* surfaces — a CLI banner, a changelog sentence, a test comment, a
design sketch, a bridge table, a positioning paragraph, a bot directive — each of
which quietly moved a claim one level up. Twenty-two such moves are recorded below;
the load-bearing ones are corrected. On JanusKey: *there is no integration to
audit.* Valence Shell neither imports JanusKey nor depends on `reversible-core`;
the integration is planned, on JanusKey's side, and the D204 ruling is a decision
rather than an artefact.

== The claim ladder, restated

This is the audit's own statement of the levels, aligned with the new
`docs/THEORY-FEED.adoc` § "Assurance boundary" so that the two documents cannot
drift apart.

[cols="1,3,3",options="header"]
|===
| Level | Earned by | Not earned by

| *Conceptual*
| prose proposing a fit
| anything at all — keep the word "inference"

| *Documented*
| a maintained bridge/orientation document
| the existence of the document. An orientation doc adds no theorem and no import.

| *Mechanised*
| a proof file importing the dependency, building in CI
| a shared vocabulary, a shared author, or a shared decision record

| *Refined*
| a machine-checked relation between the model and the executing code
| any test, fuzz target, differential oracle, extraction script or code comment
|===

And the three separations that the level ladder does not itself express:

*Proof vs warrant vs receipt.* Taken from `epistemic-types` @ `5422cd5`
(`PROOF-STATUS.adoc`, `docs/what-epistemic-is.adoc`, `docs/proof-transport.adoc`):
a `Proof` type-checks under `--safe --without-K` with zero postulates; a
`Warrant`/`Epi` is deliberately *non-factive* and holding one does not yield the
claim; `SoundWarrant` adds `sound : Evidence -> A` as a *separate* interface; and
a `Receipt` never certifies — the library proves `verifyReceiptIsGap`. The same
repo states plainly that it "is NOT a verified external system" and that cross-repo
orientation documents are not mechanised dependencies. Any valence-shell sentence
that names a warrant or receipt as if it were a proof has crossed that line.

*Algorithmic vs thermodynamic.* `F⁻¹(F(s)) = s`, with `F⁻¹` a program, is
algorithmic reversibility. Thermodynamic reversibility is about energy dissipation
and the Landauer/Bennett programme. Valence Shell has the first and not the second,
and its own `FAQ.adoc` says so. Naming the product "the thermodynamic shell" is the
error the FAQ warns about.

*Model vs machine.* `rmdir (mkdir p fs) = fs` holds of `Filesystem`, a function in
a proof assistant. The shipped `vsh` is Rust calling `libc`. Nothing in this
repository relates the two by proof.

=== What JanusKey says about the relationship

Measured in `januskey` @ `4afb710`, quoted because these are claims *about this
repository* that Valence Shell's own docs must not silently adopt:

* `ARCHITECTURE.adoc` lists three downstream applications — ochrance,
  valence-shell, aletheia — and then, under "Phase 2: valence-shell integration",
  marks it **NEXT**: wire `impl/rust-cli/` to depend on `reversible-core`, add
  `ContentStore` to `ShellState`, replace `undo_data: Option<Vec<u8>>`, add an
  A2ML emitter, replace `verification.rs` stubs.
* `crates/reversible-core/src/content_store.rs` describes `ContentStore` as "the
  shared storage backend that both januskey-cli and valence-shell use".
* `crates/reversible-core/src/lib.rs` describes the lineage
  `maa-framework (policy) → absolute-zero (theory) → januskey (PoC) → ochrance,
  valence-shell, aletheia (applications)`.

Read precisely, all three are statements of *intended* consumption. None is a
statement that consumption has happened, and none of them is a proof claim. The
user-facing asymmetry matters too: JanusKey says its own formal proofs of complete
reversibility are "pending" (`README.adoc` CAUTION), so even a landed code
dependency would not import assurance — it would import Rust.

=== What the relationship is, and is not

* *Engineering, planned.* `reversible-core` (content-addressed store, operation
  metadata, `ReversibleExecutor`) is a natural home for the undo payloads that
  `ShellState` currently keeps inline. That is a code dependency. If it lands it
  will be a Cargo line in `impl/rust-cli/Cargo.toml`, reviewed on its own merits.
* *Proof, unwritten.* The D204 ruling ("Arm A": absolute-zero factors out a
  parameterised CNO interface; valence-shell instantiates it) answers *where* the
  formal home of filesystem reversibility should be. It does not create the
  instantiation, and it does not create a januskey→valence-shell refinement.
  Under either arm, "januskey's operations refine valence-shell's model" is a
  target, not a theorem.
* *Not a matrix move.* Adding `reversible-core` as a dependency would not change
  any cell of the `docs/THEORY-FEED.adoc` matrix from C to M. M requires a proof
  import that builds; sharing a Rust crate is T at best, and T is "tooling in use",
  not "mechanised".
* *Upstream bridge is a name bridge.* `echo-types`' `EchoJanusBridge.agda` is a
  4-variant local enum over the name "Janus", drifted from JanusKey's 8-variant
  `OpKind`. It lives upstream, has no valence-shell counterpart, and does not
  involve this repository.

=== Exactly what would change this verdict

Any one of these, and this section must be rewritten:

. a `Cargo.toml` entry for `reversible-core` in `impl/rust-cli/` (engineering edge);
. an `.agda`/`.v`/`.lean`/`.idr` file under `proofs/` importing a JanusKey or
absolute-zero module, with the building CI job named;
. a theorem in either repo stating a refinement between a `jk` operation and a

Until then the correct sentence is: *"JanusKey integration is planned; Valence
Shell has no implemented dependency on it."*

== RMO: the boundary, restated

The implementation is a three-pass overwrite with `fsync`, an `unlink`, and a
directory sync on Unix, plus an appended record in `<root>/.vsh-audit.log`. The
audit residue is *unsigned* unless a key is provisioned, and the RMO path
deliberately provisions none.

Therefore, and this is the sentence every other document must agree with:
`obliterate` is a *best-effort local file-erasure primitive* on cooperating
in-place filesystems. It is not a claim that bytes are absent from copy-on-write
extents, journals, snapshots, flash translation layers, device spares, caches,
replicas, backups or remote storage, and it is not a GDPR Article 17 determination.
An abstract Z3 model of a filesystem/block mapping, and a Coq/Idris2 non-injectivity
theorem, are statements about the model. Neither reaches the device.

The canonical long form is `docs/RMO-DURABILITY-BOUNDARY.adoc`; this audit agrees
with it and found only *other* files disagreeing.

== The extraction gap: what this audit found inside it

Three facts, each measured, that sharpen the boundary rather than move it:

. *The Coq extraction is a placeholder.* `proofs/coq/extraction.v` extracts
  `Filesystem` as `unit -> unit`, commented "Placeholder". It cannot represent a
  filesystem state. The file's header used to say it "bridges the gap between
  formal proofs and real implementation"; it now says what it is.
. *The shipped CLI does not use the FFI shard.* `ffi/rust` (`vsh-ffi`) is a
  separate crate. It is not a dependency of `impl/rust-cli/` and is not referenced
  from its sources; `.github/workflows/rust-cli.yml` builds and tests it separately.
  The `README.adoc` trust diagram previously labelled the implementation layer
  "OCaml", which is neither the shipped binary nor the extraction target's language
  at runtime; it now names the Rust CLI and notes the separate crate.
. *The differential oracle is a test.* `proofs/lean4/ModelOracle.lean` +
  `impl/rust-cli/tests/model_oracle_correspondence.rs` execute the compiled proven
  model against the Rust `ShellState` and compare probes (1,218 probes over 200
  sequences, per the changelog). It is genuinely stronger than hand-written
  property tests, because the oracle *is* the proof artefact. It is still
  differential testing. `docs/LEAN4_RUST_CORRESPONDENCE.adoc` says this correctly
  in its own "What this does and does not establish" subsection, and that is the
  sentence to keep.

=== Agda: the tree did not compile at all, and now does

The headline is worse than AP-6 said and then better. When this round started,
*every* module in `proofs/agda/` failed with a parse error under
`agda <file>` -- the invocation the repository itself uses. `valence-shell.agda-lib`
was itself unparseable, because `.agda-lib` has no comment syntax and the file
opened with `--` header lines. There was therefore no compiler-produced evidence
of any kind for any Agda row: `proven-with-postulates` was a statement about
intent.

The source defects found and repaired (commit `a903c3f`, each site commented in
the file) were:

. Nested `with` under a with-clause (`... | yes refl with p path-≟ p`) in six
  modules. Agda cannot parse this layout; re-indenting only makes it *silently
  regroup* the clauses (`UnreachableClauses` plus `Incomplete pattern matching`),
  so each site was restructured onto `Dec`-passing helpers.
. `FilesystemModel.agda:118` declared a record field `parentExists` inside a
  module that already defines `parentExists` -- a hard error, masked by the
  parse error above. Same class: `isFile` (FileOperations),
  `defaultPerms` (FilesystemComposition), `isFile` (FileContentOperations).
. `open import Level using (lzero)` at `FilesystemModel.agda:21`, removed from
  `Level` in stdlib 2.x.
. `rmdir-removes-path` proved `⊥` with `λ ()`; the goal is `⊥`, not a function.
. `FilesystemComposition.reverseSequence` was `map reverseOp (reverse ops)`, and
  `reverse` is a foldl in stdlib, so the composition theorem could not reduce.
. `FilesystemEquivalence` asserted its theorems over `Reversible`, while
  `singleOpReversible` needs the default-permissions witness in `ReversibleDP`.
. `FileContentOperations` and `RMOOperations` were written against constructors
  and helper names that do not exist (`file`/`directory` rather than
  `File`/`Directory`, `getNodeType`, `delete-file`, `path-exists`, `is-file`), and
  `RMOOperations` redefined `case_of_` four times inside `where` blocks. Both
  files were rewritten against the model that exists.

=== What that does to the Agda rows, and to Grade 3

* The Agda rows are now **reproduced**: they are `proven-with-postulates` under the
  *unflagged* invocation, with one postulate (`funext`) and no holes. That is the
  first compiler-backed evidence the repository has had for them.
* They are **not** Grade 3. mathesis §5 defines Grade 3 as type-checking under the
  policy invocation, and PROOF-POLICY §2.1 pins that as
  `agda --safe --without-K --cubical-compatible`. Two independent obstacles
  remain, and they are both proof problems, not prose problems:
  **the `funext` postulate** (for `--safe`) and **K-dependent matching on
  `p ≟ p`** (for `--without-K`; any `Dec (p ≡ p)` case split needs K, because
  Agda cannot eliminate the reflexive equation on a `List String` without it).
  Escaping the second one means threading the path decision through the model
  instead of asking for it again at each site.
* Until both are settled, the Agda leg sits at *Claimed, reproduced under a
  waiver*. PROOF-POLICY §4 requires that waiver to be recorded beside the
  artefact; the ledger rows say so.

  the Yokoyama 2007 reversible language, not JanusKey, and its thermodynamics
  discussion is literature framing; neither was touched. Its GDPR and
  "every operation" claims were corrected in place (AB-23), because a paper in
  the repository is a claim surface like any other. Unrelated staleness (test
  and line counts in the abstract) was left alone.
* *`CONTINUATION_SESSION_COMPLETE.adoc`, `SESSION_COMPLETE.adoc`,
  `EXTRACTION_SUMMARY.adoc`, `docs/PHASE*`, `docs/SESSION_*`.* Dated session
  records. They say "Coq → OCaml" because that was the plan of the day. Rewriting
  them would falsify the record; they are cited here instead.
* *Historical `CHANGELOG` entries other than AB-06.* Annotated rather than
  rewritten, so the history of the claim is visible.
* *`.machine_readable/6a2/ECOSYSTEM.a2ml`.* Tempting place to record the JanusKey
  relationship, and deliberately not used: the repo's own doctrine is that A2ML is
  legacy and must not be extended (`docs/THEORY-FEED.adoc` caveats). The record
  lives in this audit, `docs/THEORY-FEED.adoc`, and `.claude/CLAUDE.md` until a
  `.k9`/`.deed` registry exists.
* *`proofs/` semantics.* No proof was edited, weakened or strengthened. AB-15
  changes comments in two extraction files only.

== Residual risk and open items

. *The refinement gap is unchanged.* This audit can only stop prose from claiming
   it is closed. F-1 (`docs/PROOF-OPEN-FRONTIER.adoc`) remains the v1.0 blocker.
. *`ffi/rust` has no consumer.* `vsh-ffi` is tested but not depended on. Either
   wire it (`impl/rust-cli/Cargo.toml`) or mark it explicitly as a shard, so the
   trust diagram does not imply a layering that does not exist.
. *No machine-readable assurance registry.* Without a `.k9`/`.deed` record, the
   C/D/T/M gradation lives in prose and will drift again. This is D-3's job
   (`Feeder:` fields): 0 of N frontier entries have one today.
. *D-1 axiom gate still absent.* The registered axioms (`funext`,
   `is_empty_dir_dec`, `axStringEqRefl`, `axBits8EqRefl`) are documented, not
   enforced on new work.
. *Bulk `.md` → `.adoc` reference sweep.* 39 files were fixed where they were
   boundary-relevant or trivially safe; many historical docs still cite old
   filenames.
. *`docs/wiki/` is published from this tree*, so its `.adoc` link fixes will reach
   the GitHub wiki on the next mirror.

* *Grade 2 is unreachable by the current arrangement.* Six parallel models are
  six statements, not one translated witness. Reaching Grade 2i/2ii needs either
  an Agda source statement per theorem with a recorded Dedukti transport or a
  recorded re-proof, per PROOF-POLICY §2 — an owner decision, not a docs change.
* *Creusot is absent.* PROOF-POLICY §1 maps first-party Rust to Creusot; the CLI
  has no contracts. Even one contract plus a Why3 receipt would give F-1 its
  first real foothold and turn a `tested-only` row into something stronger.
* *Exception notes for Isabelle/Mizar/Z3* are missing (AP-4), and keeping three
  supplementary provers without notes is exactly the kind of drift this audit
  exists to catch.
